# LoopedIn — Brand Package

## Brand platform

### Working brand name
**LoopedIn**

The shorter name is stronger for product use, app icon recall, and conversational language:
- “Check LoopedIn”
- “Drop it in LoopedInedIn”
- “I put the photos in LoopedIn”

**LoopedIn** remains a useful descriptive concept name, but **LoopedIn** is the better product brand.

### Core idea
LoopedIn is the private social calendar for real life.

It helps families and friend groups:
- know what is happening
- talk in context
- remember what mattered

### Brand promise
LoopedIn turns shared plans into shared memories.

### Positioning statement
For families and close friend groups who coordinate life across too many disconnected tools, LoopedIn is a private event-centered app that combines calendar, contextual chat, reminders, and photo memories in one polished daily-driver experience.

Unlike generic shared calendars, messy group chats, or standalone photo albums, LoopedIn keeps the plan and the memory in the same place.

## Category framing
LoopedIn lives at the intersection of:
- private social
- event coordination
- group communication
- memory capture

This is important because the product should not be presented as only:
- a calendar app
- a family organizer
- a photo-sharing tool
- a chat app

It is strongest when described as a **private social calendar** or **event-centered group app**.

## Voice and messaging

### Voice principles
1. **Clear before clever**
2. **Warm without becoming sugary**
3. **Useful without sounding corporate**
4. **Private without sounding paranoid**
5. **Modern without startup cliché**

### Voice adjectives
- human
- grounded
- polished
- light
- close
- calm

### Copy style rules
- use short direct sentences
- avoid growth-hack or creator-platform language
- avoid “community,” “followers,” “engagement,” and “audience” framing
- prefer “your group,” “your people,” and “your plans” language
- treat memories as meaningful, not sentimental fluff

### Example copy lines
- Your people, your plans, one place.
- The private social calendar for real life.
- Plan it. Chat in context. Keep the memory.
- Where events turn into memories.
- Less group-chat chaos. More shared life.
- Private by default. Useful every day.

## Visual identity direction

### Logo concept
A soft rounded symbol built from two overlapping loop forms.

It should suggest:
- connection
- return / recurrence
- intimacy
- two-way or multi-way relationships
- continuity between planning and memory

### Logo behavior
- icon-only app mark must work at small size
- wordmark should feel clean and modern
- avoid over-literal calendar or camera iconography

## Color system

### Primary palette
- **LoopedIn Plum** — `#71365D`
- **LoopedIn Coral** — `#F06F67`
- **LoopedIn Sage** — `#AAC5B1`

### Supporting neutrals
- **Porcelain** — `#FBF9F8`
- **Warm Mist** — `#F4EFEC`
- **Ink** — `#20161C`
- **Soft Graphite** — `#6D6370`

### Optional accents
- **Sunwash Gold** — `#F4C37B`
- **Sky Note** — `#C6D9F6`

### Palette rationale
Plum creates distinction and premium warmth. Coral adds energy and social life. Sage adds trust and softness. The neutral system keeps the product practical and photo-friendly.

---

## Typography direction

### Primary UI stack
System UI / SF-style / Inter-like sans

### Secondary accent
A restrained editorial serif can be used sparingly for invitations, hero moments, and memory recaps.

### Typography posture
- strong large titles
- calm utility labels
- compact metadata
- no overly technical or monospaced default feel

---

## Motion posture
LoopedIn should feel alive, not flashy.

### Motion principles
- spatial transitions between calendar and event detail
- soft spring interactions on RSVP and taps
- elegant reveal for memories and recap cards
- no noisy or novelty-first animation

### Motion brand feeling
Motion should say:
- this is personal
- this is polished
- this is easy to trust

---

## UI principles for future design work
1. Event pages are the center of gravity.
2. Photos should feel natural, not bolted on.
3. Calendar views must stay legible and fast.
4. Surfaces should feel tactile and soft.
5. Every screen should communicate privacy and closeness.
